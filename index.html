Gerunds After Prepositions 🔗

You know gerunds as subjects and objects. Now, let's learn the Golden Rule of Prepositions.

The Golden Rule

If a verb comes immediately after a preposition (in, on, at, about, of, for, with, etc.), that verb MUST be a gerund (-ing).

Adjective + Preposition

  • good / bad at drawing
  • interested in learning
  • afraid of failing
  • excited about going
  • tired of waiting

Verb + Preposition

  • think about moving
  • apologize for being late
  • believe in working hard
  • look forward to* seeing you

*Note: Here "to" acts as a preposition, not an infinitive marker!

The "L" is SILENT!

Would, Should, and Could all have the exact same vowel sound: /ʊ/ (like in "book" or "good").

Expressing Preferences ⚖️

Verbs + Gerund or Infinitive or Base Verb

A. Study the Examples

I love going out to hear live music.

I like to go to dinner and a movie.

I prefer to watch TV at home.

I prefer doing physical activities.

I'd like to do something creative.

I would prefer to have the picnic first.

I'd rather do a physical activity.

Make It Real:

Although the meaning of hate, like, love, and prefer is the same when used with an infinitive or with a gerund, the infinitive form is more commonly used in American English.

Confident Communicator

Keep Talking!

  • That's fine with me.

  • The problem is that ...
  • Sorry, but I don't really like ...
